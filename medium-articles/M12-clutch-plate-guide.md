Floor Scrubber Clutch Plate Guide: Types, Compatibility and When to Replace

By Mark Xu, Sales Director at Aikerui Cleaning Technology

The clutch plate, also called a pad driver or buckle connector, is the metal disc that connects your floor scrubber's motor shaft to the brush or pad. It looks simple, but getting the wrong type means the brush will not spin, costing you hours of downtime.

Here is everything you need to know about floor scrubber clutch plates, including types, compatibility, and replacement timing.

---

What Is a Clutch Plate?

The clutch plate transfers rotational force from the motor shaft to the brush. It is positioned between the motor and the brush, and it carries the full torque of the machine during scrubbing.

Common names: clutch plate, pad driver, buckle adapter, brush driver, center lock plate.

Material: cast iron or hardened steel, designed to withstand thousands of hours of torque.

---

The 3 Most Common Clutch Plate Types

NP-9200 Universal (2-Lug)

The most common standard on the market. Two raised nubs lock into matching slots on the brush.

Compatible with: Tennant T5/T7/T300, Viper, Nilfisk, Comac, and most Chinese OEM machines.
Center hole: 5/8 inch (16 mm).

3-Lug Center Lock

Three lugs with a center screw that secures the brush. More secure against slippage under heavy scrubbing pressure.

Compatible with: Advance, Nilfisk, and older Viper models.
Requires a matching 3-lug brush.

4-Lug Magnetic or Mechanical Buckle

Used on some European brands. Some use magnets to hold the brush, others use mechanical clips.

Compatible with: Karcher and Hako models, some Gaomei and Dulevo models.
Quick-change design, but parts are harder to source outside Europe.

---

How to Identify Your Machine's Clutch Plate Type

Step 1. Remove the brush and look at the metal plate exposed on the motor shaft.
Step 2. Count the lugs. Two lugs means NP-9200. Three lugs means center lock. Four lugs means European standard.
Step 3. Measure the center hole diameter. NP-9200 is 5/8 inch (16 mm). Others vary.
Step 4. Check for additional features such as a center screw, magnets, or clips.
Step 5. Compare with your machine model number.

When to Replace Your Clutch Plate

Bent or damaged lugs, usually from dropping the brush or hitting curbs at speed. Replace immediately, because a broken lug can damage the motor shaft.
Stripped center hole, which happens when the mounting bolt is overtightened or cross-threaded.
Rust or corrosion on the lugs, which prevents the brush from seating properly.
Worn magnetic grip on 4-lug types, when the brush falls off during operation.

A clutch plate should last several years under normal use.

---

Why Ordering the Wrong Clutch Plate Is So Common

Many buyers order a clutch plate without checking the type, get the wrong one, and then have to reorder with rush shipping. Sellers often list universal clutch plate when they mean NP-9200 only.

The rule is simple: count your lugs before ordering, not after.
