# Floor Scrubber Brush Materials Deep Dive: Nylon, PPL, Abrasive & More

**By Mark Xu | Sales Director at Aikerui Cleaning Technology**

The material of your floor scrubber brush determines how well it cleans — and how long it lasts. Pick the wrong material and you'll either scratch your floor or leave dirt behind.

Here's everything you need to know about floor scrubber brush materials, from the most common (nylon) to the most aggressive (steel wire).

---

## The 5 Brush Materials Explained

### 1. Nylon — The Everyday Workhorse

Nylon is the most widely used floor scrubber brush material. It's flexible, durable, and safe on most floor types.

| Property | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| Stiffness | Medium |
| Best for | Tile, concrete, epoxy — daily general cleaning |
| Lifespan | 6-12 months with daily use |
| Cost | $45-75 (factory-direct) |
| Bristle tip | Rounded, gentle on floors |

**When to use:** This is your default choice. If you're not sure what material you need, start with nylon. It handles 90% of cleaning jobs without damaging the floor.

### 2. Polypropylene (PPL) — The Heavy-Duty Scrubber

Polypropylene bristles are stiffer than nylon, giving more scrubbing power for stubborn dirt.

| Property | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| Stiffness | High |
| Best for | Heavy grease, oil, industrial grime |
| Lifespan | 6-9 months |
| Cost | $50-80 (factory-direct) |
| Warning | Can scratch soft floors — use on concrete only |

**When to use:** Factories, warehouses, and facilities with heavy soil buildup. If your nylon brush isn't removing the grime, switch to PPL.

### 3. Abrasive (Silicon Carbide / Tynex) — The Stripper

Abrasive brushes contain silicon carbide or Tynex particles that aggressively cut through buildup.

| Property | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| Stiffness | Very high |
| Best for | Stripping old floor finish, deep cleaning, recoating prep |
| Lifespan | 3-6 months |
| Cost | $60-90 (factory-direct) |
| Warning | NOT for daily use — damages sealed floors |

**When to use:** Occasional deep cleaning. If you're stripping wax or old finish from concrete or VCT, this is the tool.

### 4. Steel Wire — The Extreme Cleaner

Steel wire brushes are the most aggressive option, used almost exclusively on industrial concrete.

| Property | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| Stiffness | Extreme |
| Best for | Heavy debris, thick grime on industrial concrete |
| Lifespan | 4-8 months |
| Cost | $70-110 (factory-direct) |
| Warning | Will damage most finished floors |

**When to use:** Only on rough, unfinished industrial concrete. Never on tile, epoxy, wood, or sealed surfaces.

### 5. Natural Fiber (Tampico / Horsehair) — The Gentle Touch

Natural fiber brushes are the softest option, designed for sensitive surfaces.

| Property | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| Stiffness | Low |
| Best for | Polishing wood, marble, linoleum |
| Lifespan | 4-6 months |
| Cost | $50-90 (factory-direct) |
| Warning | Not for heavy soil |

**When to use:** Facilities with wood or stone floors that need a gentle clean without scratching.

## Quick Selection Chart

| Floor Type | Recommended Material |
|------------|---------------------|
| Smooth concrete | Nylon (medium) |
| Epoxy / sealed concrete | Nylon (soft-medium) |
| Tile / ceramic | Nylon (soft) |
| Wood / marble | Natural fiber or soft nylon |
| Heavy grease / oil | PPL |
| Stripping / recoating | Abrasive |
| Rough industrial concrete | Steel wire |

## How to Check If Your Brush Is Worn Out

- Bristles less than **1/2 inch** → replace (disc brush)
- Bristles bent or frayed → replace
- Machine leaves streaks → worn bristles not making contact
