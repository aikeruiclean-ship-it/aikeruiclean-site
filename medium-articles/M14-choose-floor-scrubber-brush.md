# How to Choose a Floor Scrubber Brush: Complete Buyer's Guide

**By Mark Wang | Sales Director, Aikerui**

Choosing the right floor scrubber brush is the single most important decision for your cleaning results. A wrong brush will not clean properly, damage your floor, or wear out in weeks. The right brush makes cleaning faster, protects your floor surface, and saves you money over time.

This guide walks you through the four decisions you need to make: brush type, material, size, and mounting system. By the end, you will know exactly which brush fits your machine and your floor.

---

## The Four Decisions

| Decision | What It Affects |
|----------|----------------|
| Brush type | Disc vs roller vs pad driver |
| Bristle material | Nylon, PPL, abrasive, steel wire |
| Size | Diameter must match your machine |
| Mounting system | Lugs must match your driver |

Get any of these wrong and the brush will not work correctly. Let us go through each one.

---

## Decision 1: Brush Type

### Disc brush

The most common type. A flat round brush that spins against the floor. Used on the majority of walk-behind and ride-on scrubbers. Best for smooth floors like sealed concrete, tile, and epoxy. Easy to replace, and the cheapest option.

### Roller brush (cylindrical brush)

A horizontal cylinder that spins and sweeps debris into the recovery tank while scrubbing. Found on machines like the Tennant T7 or Hako B90. Better on rough, uneven concrete. A roller machine can often clean and sweep in one pass.

### Pad driver

Not a brush but a holder. The pad driver connects the motor shaft to a cleaning pad. Used for polishing, burnishing, or light scrubbing on delicate floors.

Choose a disc brush for most facilities. Choose a roller brush if you have rough concrete or need one-pass sweeping. Choose a pad driver if you are polishing rather than scrubbing.

---

## Decision 2: Bristle Material

The material determines how aggressive the brush is on your floor.

| Material | Stiffness | Best For | Avoid On |
|----------|-----------|----------|----------|
| Nylon | Soft to medium | Daily cleaning on most floors | Nothing major |
| PPL (polypropylene) | Stiff | Heavy grease, industrial grime | Epoxy, sealed floors |
| Abrasive (SiC / tynex) | Very aggressive | Stripping finish, deep cleaning | Daily use on any floor |
| Steel wire | Most aggressive | Heavy debris on industrial concrete | Finished floors |
| Natural fiber | Softest | Polishing wood, marble, linoleum | Wet heavy soil |

Nylon is the default for 90 percent of facilities. It cleans well and is safe on most floors. Use PPL only when you have heavy grease that nylon cannot handle. Use abrasive only for occasional stripping, never daily.

---

## Decision 3: Size

The brush diameter must match your machine's cleaning width. Common sizes are:

| Machine Type | Typical Brush Diameter |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Small walk-behind | 17 inch |
| Standard walk-behind | 20 inch |
| Mid-size ride-on | 24 inch |
| Large ride-on | 28 to 32 inch |

Measure your current brush or check your machine manual. Using the wrong size means the brush will not sit flat and will not clean the full width.

---

## Decision 4: Mounting System

This is where most mistakes happen. The brush must match the lug pattern on your machine's driver.

| Mounting System | Lugs | Common Brands |
|-----------------|------|---------------|
| NP-9200 | 2 lugs | Tennant, Viper, Nilfisk, most Chinese OEM |
| Center lock | 3 lugs | Advance, older Nilfisk and Viper |
| Magnetic | 4 lugs | Karcher BD50/53, Hako B45/B70 |

Count the lugs on your current brush. Two lugs means NP-9200, which covers most machines on the market. If you are unsure, send a photo of your brush and we will confirm compatibility within 24 hours.

## Quick Selection Table

Use this table as a shortcut for your floor type.

| Floor Type | Recommended Brush | Avoid |
|------------|-------------------|-------|
| Smooth concrete | Nylon disc, medium | Abrasive for daily use |
| Epoxy or sealed | Nylon disc, soft to medium | Abrasive, steel wire |
| Tile or ceramic | Nylon disc, soft | PPL, abrasive (scratches grout) |
| Wood or marble | Natural fiber / soft pad | Abrasive, steel wire |
| Rough concrete | Cylindrical nylon or PPL | Disc brushes skip the surface |
| Heavy grease or oil | PPL stiff or abrasive | Soft nylon |

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Buying the cheapest brush. Cheap brushes often use recycled fillers that wear out fast and may scratch your floor. Check that bristles are virgin nylon or PPL with rounded tips.

Buying by brand instead of spec. A Karcher-labeled brush and a factory-direct brush with the same spec are the same product. Do not pay a 2 to 3 times brand markup for the same nylon.

Forgetting the mounting system. A beautiful brush that does not fit your driver is useless. Always confirm the lug pattern before ordering.

Replacing too late. Check bristle height monthly. Once bristles are below 12 mm, cleaning quality drops sharply and the brush can damage your floor.
