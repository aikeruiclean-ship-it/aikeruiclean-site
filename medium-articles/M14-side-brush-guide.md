Floor Scrubber Side Brush Guide: What It Does and When to Replace

By Mark Xu, Sales Director at Aikerui Cleaning Technology

The side brush, also called a gutter broom, is the smaller brush mounted on the side of a floor scrubber or sweeper. It sweeps debris from edges, corners, and along walls where the main brush cannot reach.

Here is what you need to know about floor scrubber side brushes, including what they do, compatibility, and when to replace them.

---

What Does a Side Brush Do?

The main brush of a floor scrubber cleans the main path, but it cannot reach the edges where the floor meets walls, racking, or equipment. The side brush extends the cleaning reach to these tight areas.

On scrubbers, the side brush pushes debris into the path of the main brush. On sweepers, it sweeps debris into the hopper.

Without a side brush, edges stay dirty, and over time the buildup creates a visible dirty border along your facility's walls.

---

Side Brush Types

Disc Side Brush

A flat circular brush that spins to push debris inward. The most common type on walk-behind and ride-on machines.

Cone or Cup Side Brush

A cone-shaped brush with bristles angled outward. Used on sweepers to catch debris in corners.

Cylindrical Side Brush

A tube-shaped brush used on some sweeper models. Rolls debris toward the main brush.

---

Bristle Materials

Nylon, flexible and durable, good for general sweeping
Polypropylene, stiffer, better for heavier debris
Steel wire, for the heaviest industrial debris, concrete surfaces only
A mix of materials, a common choice is nylon and steel wire combined, for balanced performance

---

Compatibility

Side brushes are not universal. They must match your machine's mounting point and bristle direction.

Brand, Common Mounting
Tennant (Sweepers and scrubbers), Disc or cone
Nilfisk, Disc
Viper, Disc
Karcher, Disc
Hako, Disc or cone

To check compatibility, count the mounting holes on your old side brush and measure the diameter. Send us a photo and we will confirm within 24 hours.

---

Signs Your Side Brush Needs Replacing

Edges of the floor are no longer being cleaned.
The brush bristles are worn to half their original length.
Bristles are bent, broken, or missing.
The brush no longer holds its shape.

Side brushes typically last 4 to 8 months with daily use, depending on the surface and debris load.
