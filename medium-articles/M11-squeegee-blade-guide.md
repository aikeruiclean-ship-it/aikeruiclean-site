# Floor Scrubber Squeegee Blade Guide: Types, Materials & When to Replace

**By Mark Xu | Sales Director at Aikerui Cleaning Technology**

If your floor scrubber leaves streaks or water behind, the squeegee blades are almost always the culprit. This simple rubber strip is the last line of defense between your machine and a clean, dry floor.

Here is everything you need to know about floor scrubber squeegee blades, including types, materials, compatibility, and replacement timing.

---

## What Is a Squeegee Blade?

The squeegee blade is the rubber strip at the back of your floor scrubber. After the brush scrubs and the vacuum lifts the dirty water, the squeegee wipes the remaining moisture off the floor so it dries clean.

Why it matters: a worn or wrong squeegee blade means streaky, wet floors, which creates a safety hazard and wastes labor on re-cleaning.

---

## The 2 Main Materials

### Natural Rubber (NR)

Natural rubber squeegee blades are the most common type.

| Property | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| Flexibility | High, conforms to the floor surface |
| Best for | Smooth floors such as concrete, tile, and epoxy |
| Lifespan | 3 to 6 months |
| Cost | $10 to $25 at factory-direct pricing |
| Warning | Softer material wears faster on rough surfaces |

### Polyurethane (PU)

Polyurethane blades are stiffer and more durable than natural rubber.

| Property | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| Flexibility | Medium, stiffer and more durable |
| Best for | Rough or uneven floors and high-traffic areas |
| Lifespan | 6 to 12 months |
| Cost | $15 to $35 at factory-direct pricing |
| Warning | Can chatter on very smooth floors |

Quick rule: choose NR for smooth floors for a better wipe, and PU for rough floors for better wear.

## Blade Types by Shape

| Type | Use |
|------|-----|
| Straight blade | Standard for most machines |
| Curved blade | Follows the machine curvature for better contact |
| Double blade | Two strips for better water pickup on wide machines |

## Signs Your Squeegee Blade Needs Replacing

1. Streaks left on the floor, which means the worn edge cannot wipe clean
2. Water puddles behind the machine, which means the blade is not making full contact
3. Visible wear such as rounded or nicked edges
4. The machine chatters instead of gliding smoothly
5. Hardened or cracked rubber from age or chemical damage

---

## How to Extend Squeegee Blade Life

| Tip | Benefit |
|-----|---------|
| Rotate blades if they are reversible | Even wear and double the lifespan |
| Clean blades daily | Remove grit that abrades the rubber |
| Store the machine with the squeegee up | Avoid flat-spotting the rubber |
| Use the correct blade material | NR on smooth floors, PU on rough floors |
| Check alignment monthly | Proper angle means even contact |
