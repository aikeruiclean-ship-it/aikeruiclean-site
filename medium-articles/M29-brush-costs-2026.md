# Floor Scrubber Brush Prices in 2026: What You Should Really Pay

If you own a floor scrubber, replacement brushes are your most frequent equipment expense. And here is the uncomfortable truth: most buyers pay two to three times what a brush actually costs, simply because they buy from the dealer instead of the source.

I manufacture these brushes. This is the honest pricing picture for 2026.

## The Real Numbers

Here is what brushes cost depending on where you buy:

| Brush Type | From the Factory | From a Dealer |
|------------|:----------------:|:--------------:|
| Disc brush (17-24 inch) | 45 to 75 USD | 100 to 200 USD |
| Roller brush | 55 to 95 USD | 120 to 300 USD |
| Side brush | 15 to 30 USD | 40 to 80 USD |
| Abrasive / stripping brush | 50 to 85 USD | 110 to 220 USD |

Same specification. Same cleaning performance. The difference is the brand label and the distribution layers between you and the production line.

## Why the Gap Exists

The machine brands that sell you brushes rarely make them. They buy from brush factories, apply their branding, and sell through a dealer network that adds its own margin. By the time a 50 dollar brush reaches you, it has passed through two or three hands.

None of that markup makes the brush clean better. It pays for logistics and branding, not bristle quality.

## What Actually Determines Brush Quality

Not the brand. Three things matter:

Bristle material. Virgin nylon is the daily workhorse. PPL is stiffer for grease and oil. Abrasive blends are for stripping. Cheap brushes often use recycled filler that sheds bristles and wears out fast.

Bristle density. Count the tufts. Dense, even packing means a longer life and better cleaning. This is where budget brushes cut corners.

Backing plate. The plate that connects to your machine must be true, or the brush will wobble and wear unevenly.

A quality factory brush at 60 dollars will outlast a cheap dealer brush at 40 dollars, and clean better the whole time.

## How to Budget Per Machine

Plan for brush spend based on how hard you run your machines:

Light duty. Two to three shifts a week on smooth sealed floors. About 200 to 400 dollars per machine per year.

Daily duty. Smooth floors, daily use. Budget 400 to 600 dollars per machine per year.

Heavy duty. Rough concrete or constant operation. Expect 600 to 1,000 dollars per machine per year.

Buying two or three brushes at once usually earns a volume discount, and you always have a spare ready.

## How to Buy Without Overpaying

Order by specification, not by brand name. A brush fits your machine if three things match: the diameter, the mounting system (the NP-9200 2-lug standard covers most machines), and the bristle material. If those match, the brush works, regardless of the logo.

Send the supplier your machine model and the OEM part number from your current brush. A good factory will confirm compatibility before you order, not after.

## The Bottom Line

A floor scrubber brush should cost you 45 to 95 dollars, not 100 to 250. If you are paying dealer prices, you are funding a distribution chain you do not need.
