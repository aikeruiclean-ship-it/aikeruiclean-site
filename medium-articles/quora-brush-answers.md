## Q1: What type of brush is best for my floor scrubber?

**Answer:**

The best brush depends on your floor type and what you are cleaning, not on the brand of your machine. Here is a simple breakdown:

Nylon medium brush. The default for most facilities. Safe on concrete, tile, and epoxy. Cleans daily dirt without scratching. If you only remember one option, remember this one.

Nylon soft brush. For polished, sealed, or coated floors like epoxy or sealed concrete. The softer bristles protect the finish.

PPL stiff brush. For heavy grease, oil, and industrial grime on unsealed concrete. More aggressive scrubbing power, but do not use it on soft floors.

Abrasive brush (silicon carbide or tynex). For stripping old finish or deep cleaning. Use occasionally, not daily, because it removes material from the floor.

Steel wire brush. For rough industrial concrete with heavy debris. The most aggressive option.

A 17 or 20 inch nylon medium brush covers about 90 percent of cleaning situations. If you are unsure, measure your current brush diameter and count the lugs. Two lugs means the NP-9200 standard, which fits most Tennant, Nilfisk, Viper, and Chinese OEM machines.

## Q2: When should I replace my floor scrubber brush?

**Answer:**

Replace the brush when cleaning quality drops, not on a fixed date. These are the signs:

Streaks or a film of dirt left after cleaning. If a fresh squeegee blade does not fix it, the brush is worn.

Bristles are flattened or broken. Upright, firm bristles clean. Flattened bristles just push dirt around.

Bristle height is below 12 mm. A new brush is typically 20 to 25 mm. Once it wears past halfway, cleaning power drops fast.

The machine vibrates more than usual. Worn bristles make the brush spin unevenly.

You can see the backing plate through the bristles. At that point the brush is not just ineffective, it can scratch the floor.

For daily use on smooth floors, expect 3 to 6 months. On rough concrete, 1 to 3 months. Light use can stretch it to 6 to 12 months.

Running a worn brush costs more than replacing it. You use more chemical, the motor works harder, and the floor never actually gets clean. A replacement brush is 45 to 75 USD factory-direct, versus 100 to 250 USD at a dealer.

## Q3: Are all floor scrubber brushes interchangeable between brands?

**Answer:**

No. The brush must match both the size and the mounting system of your machine. But the good news is that most brands share a small number of standards.

Size. Diameter is the first thing. A 20 inch machine needs a 20 inch brush. The machine manual tells you the size, or you can measure the old brush.

Mounting system. This is where brands differ:

NP-9200, 2-lug. The most common standard. Fits Tennant, Viper, Nilfisk SC series, Comac, and most Chinese OEM machines.

3-lug center lock. Common on Advance and older Nilfisk and Viper models.

4-lug magnetic. Used on Karcher BD50/53/B90 and Hako B45/B70/B75R.

Roller brushes fit by diameter and length instead of lugs. You need the exact dimensions for your model.

So the answer is: brushes are not universal, but the market has consolidated around a few standards. If your machine uses the NP-9200 2-lug standard, you have many compatible options at factory prices instead of paying the brand premium.

## Q4: What is the difference between a disc brush and a roller brush?

**Answer:**

Both clean floors, but they work differently and suit different machines and conditions.

Disc brush. A flat round brush that spins against the floor. It is the most common type, used on the majority of walk-behind and ride-on scrubbers. Best on smooth, even surfaces like sealed concrete, tile, and epoxy. Cheap and easy to replace.

Roller brush (cylindrical brush). A horizontal cylinder that spins and sweeps debris into the recovery tank while scrubbing. Found on machines like the Tennant T7 or Hako B90. Better on rough, uneven concrete because the bristles contact more surface area, and the bristle wear is more even. A roller machine can often clean and sweep in one pass, removing the need for a separate sweeping step.

The practical differences:

Disc brushes cost less and fit more machines. Roller brushes handle rough floors better and last longer on them.

Roller machines are usually more expensive than disc machines with the same cleaning width.

If your floor is smooth, a disc brush machine is the sensible choice. If you clean rough concrete with debris, a roller machine saves labor in the long run.

## Q5: How much do floor scrubber replacement brushes cost?

**Answer:**

A disc brush for a floor scrubber typically costs:

Dealer price. 100 to 250 USD for a 17 to 24 inch nylon or PPL brush.

Factory-direct price. 45 to 75 USD for the same brush, same material and specification.

Roller brushes are more: 120 to 300 USD at dealers, 55 to 95 USD factory-direct.

Why the gap? The cleaning equipment brands do not make their own brushes. They source them from factories, add the brand logo, and mark them up 2 to 3 times. Buying direct from the manufacturer gives you the same product without the markup.

A few notes on getting the right price:

Match your diameter and lug pattern, or you will buy the wrong brush and pay return shipping.

Do not buy the cheapest unknown brush. Check that it uses virgin nylon or PPL, not recycled fillers, and that bristle tips are rounded so they do not scratch the floor.

Buying 2 to 3 brushes at once usually gets a bulk discount, and you always have a spare.
